After-Hours Config: six redesigns

The same config in six shapes, every one drawn on the sample with every field filled in. Each keeps all the developers' fields; what changes is how the data is shown and where you change it. The references are from Mobbin.

A

Two-column settings

The idea

A classic settings page: each section's name and what it is for on the left, its answers on the right, every value visible and changed in place.

Good at

Nothing to learn and nothing hidden; it reads top to bottom like the developers' form, only grouped and aligned.

From

Better Stack, Sentry, Clockwise. Risk: long; it does not say what happens to a ticket.

    B

    When, if, then

    The idea

    The config is one rule, read like an automation: when a ticket arrives out of hours, if it is covered and urgent, then call in order, otherwise do the routine thing. Every highlighted word is a control.

    Good at

    One look says exactly what AiMY does, in the same shape as the automations people already know.

    From

    Productboard, Employment Hero, ClickUp. Risk: long lists (10 signs) need to fold behind a count.

      C

      Canvas and panel

      The idea

      The path as nodes on a canvas, forking at urgency into Routine and Calls. Clicking a node opens its editor in a panel on the right, like an escalation path in incident.io.

      Good at

      The path is the picture; editing never moves it. Familiar to anyone who has built an automation.

      From

      incident.io, HoneyBook, Attio. Risk: wide; on a phone the panel becomes its own screen.

      D

      The week first

      The idea

      The week is the page: a calendar with the team's shifts as blocks and everything else in colour as AiMY's time. Drag or click a block to change a shift; the rest of the config sits in a column beside it.

      Good at

      The question asked most ("is AiMY on tonight?") is answered by a glance at the calendar.

      From

      Zendesk, User Interviews. Risk: you turned bars down for hours; this is a calendar, but close.

      E

      The call sequence

      The idea

      What matters at 2 am is who gets called and when, so the page is that sequence: the checks, then each person as a step with the wait between them as a chip. The rules sit in a column on the right.

      Good at

      The timing is visible: you can count that Mona is called about 40 minutes after Yara.

      From

      Better Stack, Unify, Rox. Risk: urgency and hours become side settings.

        F

        Summary rows and a sheet

        The idea

        A short list, one row per section with its answer in a line. A row opens a sheet on the right to change it; on a phone the sheet covers the list.

        Good at

        The calmest page: seven lines say the whole config, and only one section is ever being edited.

        From

        Turo, Front. Risk: one line hides detail until you open the row.
